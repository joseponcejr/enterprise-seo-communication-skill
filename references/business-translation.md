# Translate the business question

The business reader usually needs a decision or understanding, not a substitute acronym. Choose the explanation that fits the actual context. These are useful defaults, not claims about every executive.

## Reader and job

- Executive sponsor: What matters, why now, what should we prioritize, what decision is mine?
- Finance/investment: What is the investment, scope, alternative, exposure, assumption, and supported return?
- Marketing leader: Which demand or audience are we reaching, how do channels relate, and what changes next?
- Delivery owner: Which exact asset, defect, dependency, and acceptance condition?
- Enterprise buyer on a service page: Is this for my situation, what does it solve, why trust it, and what is the next step?

Keep the audience's competence intact. Do not write as though technical detail is inherently unwelcome. Move detail behind the business point, not out of the evidence trail.

## Working methods

### Opportunity before execution

When the decision is where to invest, distinguish the company's current footprint from the external opportunity. Useful evidence can include category demand, customer fit, competing alternatives, feasibility, constraints, and existing performance. A company with no content on a topic may have little Search Console data; that alone does not show there is no demand. Tool estimates are estimates, not guaranteed traffic.

Say what is known, how it affects the choice, and what specific information remains missing. Do not invent a strategic priority to finish the narrative. If the user only asks to translate a phrase, do not demand a complete market study.

### Finding to decision

Use the portions that apply: finding, business implication, recommendation, supporting evidence, next action or decision. Do not force all five into every sentence. A straightforward update may need no decision.

A business implication can explain a mechanism without claiming measured impact. For example, a correction to preferred-page signals gives search engines clearer direction about which page to consider. It does not prove that traffic increased. Use a separate explanatory sentence when the implication is not part of the source finding.

### High-level point, support, return to action

Start with the main point. Provide enough evidence to defend it. Return to the proposed or agreed action. Keep qualifications that change the main point near it; detailed diagnostics can move to supporting notes. A long activity list is not an executive story.

### Make the work legible

Report separately: completed, underway, proposed next, blocked/dependent, and decision needed. Name owners only when supplied. Never turn hours spent into business value without an explanation of the work and evidence. Never imply completed work merely to make progress sound stronger.

### Make a recommendation without borrowing authority

Where asked and supported, provide the exact choice, recommendation, and tradeoff. Resolve retrievable factual questions before asking the client, using only authorized sources. Missing decision authority stays missing. A recommendation is not an approved plan.

### Two jobs for a page

Human clarity helps readers assess relevance, trust, and action. Explicit business information may help answer systems represent facts and relationships. These are different jobs, not one guaranteed mechanism. Found, cited, recommended, and chosen are useful separate observations, not a guaranteed sequential funnel.

## Terminology patterns

Use these as explanations, not automatic replacements. Preserve tense and status from the actual input.

- Semantic triples: explicit subject-relationship-object statements. For a general reader, explain which business facts need clarity. Not every short sentence is a formal triple.
- Entity clarity: make the business, services, audiences, locations, and relevant relationships explicit and consistent. Do not promise AI inclusion.
- Canonical tags: signals identifying preferred pages. Keep the technical term in an implementation handoff; search engines can select a different canonical.
- Indexability: whether a page can be considered for inclusion in a search index. Eligibility is not actual indexing or ranking.
- Content syndication: distributing content through other channels. State owned, paid, or independent status where material.
- Link building: obtaining or creating links, with the actual method identified. Do not substitute earned endorsement or AI citation.
- Citation-worthy content: a proposed quality goal. Explain the useful original evidence, clarity, or answer it provides rather than assert future citation.
- Third-party validation: independent substantiation only when independence and the specific supporting claim are established. Publication on another domain alone does not prove independence.
- Share of voice: define channel, sample, unit, denominator, time window, and brand rules. A share of monitored answers, a share of ten search results, and market share are not interchangeable.
- Non-brand traffic: traffic for queries classified as non-branded under a stated method. It is not automatically new-customer acquisition.
- AI visibility: a measured or sampled presence in answers, with models/prompts/dates specified. It is not attributable revenue.

## Style rules

Use direct verbs and concrete nouns. Avoid “maximize brand management,” “synergistic optimization,” or “unlock exponential growth” when the actual action is clearer. Keep required technical identifiers exact. If a phrase is already clear, say so and retain it. Do not claim to know how a particular stakeholder thinks without source-backed feedback.
